Having just finished a long Mothership campaign, and looking to start something new with two separate groups I polled my players. They all seemed keen on some classic d&d antics. 

(Un)fortunately for them I've never played d&d, ever since I picked up a pathfinder rulebook I've had an intense distrust of rules systems that read more like a tactical wargame. In my mind the two hobbies are both great fun, but scratch very different itches. 

Eager not to disappoint my players, and because the idea of running a classic dungeon crawling, sword and sorcery campaign appealed, I started looking around for a rule set that would fit my rules light preference while still feeling like d&d. 

Enter [Shadowdark](https://www.thearcanelibrary.com/pages/shadowdark). Most easily described as basic d&d from the 80s but updated with modern sensibilities. After reading and watching a few reviews I decided it was worth taking a punt on the system and picked up a copy from [Dungeonland](https://www.dungeonland.co.uk), a small UK based retailer. Though I could have made do with a pdf, I very much enjoy having a physical copy as it means I don't have to be using a screen when I'm learning a rule system or planning for games. 

I started prepping a game and ran straight into an unexpected problem.

Both games I run are online, using foundry as a virtual tabletop. About 30 minutes of experimenting with the Shadowdark module for foundry convinced me that I would lose about 50% of my enjoyment due to the clunkiness of the module. Having not written as much frontend code at work recently I decided that the logical solution was to write my own VTT. 

With that problem neatly solved (spoiler I spent about 30 hours writing the VTT, but a story for another time) I was ready to run a campaign. You can view the campaign primer [here](https://iendjinn-ga.me/2026/10/26/Shadowdark-Campaign.html).

So what do I think of Shadowdark? Well after a total of 5 sessions between the two groups I'm thoroughly enjoying it. The balance is just right between enough crunch to keep PCs distinct, and their players keen to gain levels, and light enough that I as the GM don't feel confined by the rules.

I'll aim to do a full review of the system once I've run more sessions and can really comment on how it plays across a campaign.
